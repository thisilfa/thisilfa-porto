# Art Direction

This document proposes three genuinely different visual directions for the same verified content. None requires invented proof, a new product, or a change to factual project claims. All directions assume bilingual parity and accessible responsive behavior.

## Shared strategic brief

The portfolio should communicate:

- applied AI systems judgment, not only model familiarity;
- operational computer-vision depth supported by LLM, retrieval, backend, and deployment experience;
- clear role and contribution boundaries;
- a human, authored professional identity;
- confidence without startup-style hype.

The visual system should make three flagship projects immediately distinguishable and make the remaining archive fast to scan.

### Evaluation boundary

TasteSkill can inspect implemented pages in a real browser, run copy/consistency/performance gates, and compare responsive captures. It cannot produce a defensible automated pass/fail score for an unbuilt art direction. The evaluations below therefore apply TasteSkill's documented principles to the proposals and are explicitly qualitative. No direction is reported as having passed a browser gate. A browser-scored comparison belongs in the prototype phase after approval.

## Direction 01 — Editorial Engineering

### Concept

A personal engineering journal: warm, deliberate, and strongly authored. Case studies read like carefully edited technical essays with figures, margin notes, and evidence plates.

### Visual language

- Warm paper background, carbon text, ochre/burnished brass accent, muted clay for limitations.
- Source Serif 4 for display/editorial statements; Source Sans 3 for reading and navigation; mono only inside diagrams/code labels if required.
- Asymmetric editorial grid with generous margins, figure numbers, rules, and pull statements.
- Portrait photography remains prominent; system evidence is framed like publication figures rather than app mockups.
- Motion: one restrained hero type entrance and figure-caption transitions. No ambient animation.

### Homepage idea

Hero combines portrait, a concise applied-AI positioning line, and a small “currently working across” index. The next screen opens the SIMS case with a large evidence figure and a precise operational claim. CSSD and Prophesy follow as distinct editorial chapters, then a compact professional path.

### Case-study expression

- One-screen engineering abstract.
- Numbered figures and captions.
- Margin annotations for role, status, evaluation boundary, and confidentiality.
- Long prose remains comfortable, but every major section is broken by evidence.

### Strengths

- Closest to the existing identity, so it preserves recognizable brand equity.
- Excellent for nuance, limitations, and authored technical writing.
- Avoids generic AI aesthetics.

### Risks

- Can still feel more like a writer/researcher than an engineer if diagrams are not sufficiently concrete.
- The existing serif-led design already occupies part of this territory; the redesign may feel evolutionary rather than decisively new.
- Long case studies could remain text-heavy.

### TasteSkill evaluation

- Hierarchy: strong if each screen has one figure and one argument.
- Typography: distinctive and readable, but must keep interface labels out of the serif.
- Composition: strong editorial potential; asymmetry must retain equal outer edges.
- Professional credibility: high for research/analysis, medium-high for systems implementation.
- Outcome: **viable, but not the recommended primary direction.**

## Direction 02 — System Evidence (recommended)

### Concept

A contemporary engineering dossier built around inputs, transformations, decisions, and outcomes. It is technical without becoming cyber-themed, and personal without relying on lifestyle imagery.

### Visual language

- Mineral off-white ground with graphite text.
- Deep mineral blue for links and implemented paths, data green for verified/evaluated states, evaluation amber for experimental or constrained states, and rust only for risks/limitations.
- IBM Plex Sans for display, interface, and body; IBM Plex Mono for labels, figures, statuses, and data. Two families, clear responsibilities.
- A strict 12-column grid, thin structural rules, large plain type, and project-specific system diagrams.
- Evidence plates use real/sanitized assets where available; otherwise generalized diagrams are explicitly labeled.
- Motion: a hero system line assembles once; case-study diagrams may reveal paths on intent/scroll only when the static state is complete and reduced-motion-safe.

### Homepage idea

The hero states “Applied AI systems for real operational constraints” with computer vision as the strongest specialization, not the only category. Beside it is a compact system map connecting perception, evaluation, APIs, retrieval, and deployment. The next screen is a full evidence plate for SIMS. Two smaller but materially different plates introduce CSSD and Prophesy. A short capability matrix then maps demonstrated capabilities back to specific work rather than listing tools in isolation.

### Case-study expression

- Engineering brief: problem, role, status, environment, outcome/evidence, and limitation.
- A responsive architecture map with semantic states: implemented, contributed, contextual, unverified.
- Decision records pairing constraint → choice → consequence.
- Evaluation section separating operational metric, model metric, test set, known failure, and unmeasured question.
- Evidence gallery of sanitized interfaces, outputs, data shapes, or generalized flows.

### Strengths

- Makes the portfolio’s strongest differentiator—truthful engineering evidence—visible.
- Supports computer vision, RAG, APIs, and edge systems without looking like a generic dashboard.
- Scales cleanly from a recruiter scan to technical depth.
- Can retain the warmth and ochre memory of the current site through evaluation amber and portrait treatment.

### Risks

- Requires asset collection and careful diagram design; weak placeholder diagrams would undermine the concept.
- Too many labels or rules could turn it into documentation rather than a personal portfolio.
- Sanitized artifacts must be reviewed for confidentiality.

### TasteSkill evaluation

- Hierarchy: strongest of the three because every section has one named engineering claim and proof object.
- Typography: one technical family plus mono keeps navigation and data coherent.
- Composition: strict grid resolves current cross-page inconsistency and desktop placement risk.
- Color/contrast: semantic color has a job and can be tested systematically.
- Responsive design: diagrams can be deliberately redrawn as vertical flows, not merely shrunk.
- Accessibility: strongest path to 16px text, 44px targets, semantic figures, and non-visual fallbacks.
- Professional credibility: highest for the intended AI/ML engineering audience.
- Outcome: **recommended.**

## Direction 03 — Swiss Technical Minimalism

### Concept

A terse, high-contrast professional index. The site behaves like a precise catalogue: strong typography, hard alignment, sparse color, and almost no ornamental layer.

### Visual language

- Near-white, black, and one safety-red or cobalt accent.
- Archivo for display/body; IBM Plex Mono for metadata.
- Dense but orderly 12-column grid, visible numbering, square corners, and compact lists.
- Project archive resembles an exhibition index or technical catalogue, with full-bleed evidence appearing only for featured work.
- Motion: minimal. Fast hover/focus state changes and one opening line movement only.

### Homepage idea

An oversized name/role block, followed by a numbered list of three systems and their strongest verified evidence. The professional path is a compact timeline. Contact information is treated as a utility band rather than a large closing campaign.

### Case-study expression

- Compact fact table first.
- Strong numbered sections.
- Diagrams are monochrome with one accent state.
- Long prose is edited aggressively into short blocks and decision tables.

### Strengths

- Excellent scanning and strong visual consistency.
- Makes the project archive efficient on mobile.
- Clear break from generic AI aesthetics.

### Risks

- Could erase the warmth and personality already established by the portrait and cream palette.
- Severe minimalism can read as design-forward but emotionally distant.
- Monochrome diagrams may not communicate contribution/evaluation states as efficiently.

### TasteSkill evaluation

- Hierarchy and grid: excellent.
- Typography: strong if line lengths and density stay controlled.
- Composition: risks becoming list-heavy and visually undernourished.
- Professional credibility: high, but personal differentiation is lower than Direction 02.
- Outcome: **strong alternative for a more austere identity.**

## Direction comparison

| Criterion | Editorial Engineering | System Evidence | Swiss Technical Minimalism |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Preserves current warmth | High | Medium-high | Low-medium |
| Shows technical depth quickly | Medium-high | High | High |
| Supports nuanced limitations | High | High | Medium-high |
| Project visual differentiation | Medium-high | High | Medium |
| Mobile transformation potential | Medium | High | High |
| Personal authorship | High | High | Medium |
| Risk of generic AI styling | Low | Low | Low |
| Asset/diagram effort | Medium | High | Medium |
| Recruiter scan speed | Medium | High | High |
| Recommended confidence | Good | Best | Good alternative |

## Recommendation

Choose **Direction 02 — System Evidence**, with two constraints:

1. Retain the current site’s warmth through the portrait, mineral off-white background, and evaluation amber rather than replacing it with a dark technical theme.
2. Borrow Editorial Engineering’s figure captions and narrative pacing so the system does not become a sterile dashboard.

This is a recommendation, not an approved implementation decision. Direction 01 remains the closest conservative evolution; Direction 03 is the strongest option if a more severe and minimal identity is desired.

## Required assets before implementation

No asset below should be invented. Each must be provided, safely recreated, or explicitly labeled as generalized:

- Sanitized SIMS shelf/camera frame or counting-output example.
- Sanitized CSSD instrument/mask/retrieval example.
- Sanitized Prophesy retrieval trace, workflow, or result example.
- Confirmation that the current portrait may continue to be used.
- Resume PDF source of truth.
- Approved project statuses, dates, role language, and metric definitions.
- Confidentiality review for every artifact.

If real product UI is unavailable, use project-specific architecture/evaluation figures. Do not fabricate dashboards, client screens, or production telemetry.
